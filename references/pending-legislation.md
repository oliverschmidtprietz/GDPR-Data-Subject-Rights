# Pending Legislation — Digital Omnibus (NOT law)

The Commission's Digital Omnibus proposal (COM(2025) 837 final, 19 Nov 2025, procedure
2025/0360(COD)) would amend the GDPR. **Apply current law; flag the proposal in one
sentence where relevant; never blend proposed text into the analysis.**

## Status (last verified 2026-10-02)

Still not adopted, still in European Parliament committee stage:

- **European Parliament:** jointly assigned to ITRE and LIBE. Co-rapporteurs (Aura
  Salla, ITRE; Marina Kaljurand, LIBE) published a draft report 22 June 2026; the
  committees discussed it jointly 13 July 2026; over 1,750 amendments were tabled by
  the 15 July 2026 deadline. No committee vote or plenary adoption as of this
  verification.
- **Council:** the Commission presented the proposal to the Antici Group 21 Nov 2025.
  A Council general-approach vote scheduled for 26 June 2026 was **cancelled** over
  unresolved disagreements; work continues under the Irish Council Presidency.
- **Consultative bodies:** EESC opinion adopted 18 Mar 2026; Committee of the Regions
  opinion adopted 7 May 2026.
- No trilogue has started. Adoption before 2027 remains unlikely.

Re-check procedure 2025/0360(COD) on EUR-Lex / the Legislative Observatory before any
major deliverable that turns on this status.

## DSR-relevant items in the tabled text

- **Art. 12(5) replaced**: new ground to refuse/charge for **Art. 15 requests** abused
  "for purposes other than the protection of their data", plus a lowered proof bar for
  "excessive" — would partly reverse C-307/22 motive-irrelevance. The Council
  compromise under discussion keeps only a tightened version requiring *demonstrated
  abusive intention* (close to the C-526/24 case law).
- **Art. 22 rewritten** as a permission rule with a broadened contract exception
  (Council compromise deletes this).
- **Relative "personal data" definition** (Art. 4(1)) + pseudonymisation implementing
  acts (new Art. 41a) — would gate all DSRs off for entities that cannot identify the
  person (Council compromise deletes this).
- **New Arts. 88a-88c**: machine-readable consent-refusal and Art. 21(2) objection
  signals controllers must honour; unconditional objection to AI-training processing
  (Council compromise deletes these).

Untouched by the tabled text: deadlines and modalities (Art. 12(1)-(4), (6)-(8)), Arts.
14-21 as written, Art. 23.
