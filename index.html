§ 01 · CAPABILITIES

SIX PRIMITIVES.
One SKILL.

Each capability is documented separately, tested separately, and called by the workflow at the right moment. Compose them or invoke individually.

01
RIGHT-BY-RIGHT ROUTING
Lean orchestrator routes any incoming request: Art. 15 access and Art. 17 erasure as deep tracks; rectification, restriction, portability, objection, and Art. 22 in a compact track. A 'delete everything and tell me what you have' email is read as BOTH rights.
02
15 LEGAL PRECISION POINTS
Hard rules where training knowledge is commonly imprecise: the clock starts at receipt on any channel, first copy is free, motive is irrelevant, recipients are named specifically on request (C-154/21).
03
VERIFIED CJEU CORPUS
All 14 cited CJEU judgments verified against primary sources — including the post-cutoff C-526/24 abusive-first-request doctrine, reconciled in writing with C-307/22 motive-irrelevance.
04
ERASURE TWO-STEP TEST
Art. 17(1) ground first, THEN Art. 17(3) exceptions. Statutory retention (AO § 147, HGB § 257) defeats erasure only for the retained records — Art. 18 restriction covers the remainder. Backups staged and disclosed, never silently ignored.
05
REFUSAL HIGH BAR
Art. 12(5) manifestly-unfounded/excessive with the burden on the controller; extension is +2 months, declared within the first month, with reasons. Broad scope alone is not excessive — ask the data subject to specify.
06
OMNIBUS WATCH
Digital Omnibus (COM(2025) 837, procedure 2025/0360(COD) — still in EP committee, no Council general approach) tracked with a current-law-first rule: apply the GDPR as it stands, flag the proposal in one sentence, never blend proposed text into the analysis.
§ 02 · WORKFLOW

FROM INPUT
to ARTEFACT.

Eight structured steps. The human stays accountable; the skill carries the structure, the citations, and the document trail.

01
Disclaimer & intake
Non-blocking disclaimer, then intake: which right(s) the request invokes (read generously), receipt date on any channel, requester relationship, jurisdiction and sector.
02
Identity check
Only on concrete Art. 12(6) doubts — proportionate verification via the existing authentication channel, not ID-card copies by default. Verification pauses nothing.
03
Clock confirmation
One calendar month from receipt (Art. 12(3)) — not from verification, not from when it reaches the privacy team. The concrete deadline date is computed and stated.
04
Right-by-right assessment
Deep track for access (EDPB 01/2022 scoping, copy test per C-487/21) and erasure (ground/exception test); compact track for Art. 16/18/20/21/22.
05
Exceptions & restrictions
Art. 12(5) high-bar tests; rights of others as targeted redaction, never blanket denial (Art. 15(4)); Art. 23 national restrictions with BDSG §§ 32/33/34/35/36/37 carve-outs.
06
Downstream duties
Art. 19 recipient notification unless impossible or disproportionate; Art. 17(2) reasonable steps toward other controllers including search engines; consent withdrawal propagates (C-129/21 Proximus).
07
Response draft
Grant, partial grant with redaction reasoning, reasoned refusal, or extension notice — bilingual EN/DE skeletons, .docx via the document skills.
08
Register entry
Every request gets a DSR log row. Accountability (Art. 5(2)) means the file shows WHY a refusal or redaction was justified.

§ 09 · README.MD

Data Subject Rights — Deployment Guide

GDPR data subject rights handling (Art. 12–23): intake and triage of incoming requests, right-by-right assessment, exception and national-restriction analysis, and response drafting — access (Art. 15) and erasure (Art. 17) as deep tracks; rectification, restriction, portability, objection, and automated-decision rights covered in a compact track.

What it does

  • Triage: which right(s) a request invokes, concrete deadline computation, proportionate identity verification
  • Access: full Art. 15 scoping per EDPB Guidelines 01/2022 and current CJEU case law (recipient naming, copy scope, motive irrelevance, third-party redaction)
  • Erasure: two-step ground/exception test; statutory retention limited to covered records and periods under Art. 17(3)(b), with purpose limitation, access controls, and scheduled deletion. A formal Art. 18 restriction requires an independent Art. 18(1) ground; BDSG § 35(3) covers by-laws-based or contractual, not statutory, retention periods. Also covers backups and Art. 17(2)/19 downstream duties
  • Refusals, fees, and extensions with the Art. 12(5) high-bar tests and mandatory remedies notices
  • Deliverables: bilingual (EN/DE) response-letter skeletons, DSR register schema, .docx/.xlsx output via the document skills

Installation

Copy or symlink the skill folder into your Claude Code skills directory:

ln -s /path/to/claude-skills/skills/data-subject-rights ~/.claude/skills/data-subject-rights
